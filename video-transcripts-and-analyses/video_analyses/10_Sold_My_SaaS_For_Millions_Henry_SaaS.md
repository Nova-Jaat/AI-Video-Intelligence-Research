## 2. Executive Summary
Henry SaaS details his multi-year journey building a lean B2B software-as-a-service (SaaS) business from scratch to a multi-million-dollar acquisition on Acquire.com. He stresses that successful solo-founder software is rarely a revolutionary breakthrough; it is almost always a **boring, highly specific workflow automation tool for an established corporate department** that replaces tedious manual Excel work.

---

## 3. The Money Math & Economic Reality
**The Multiples of SaaS Valuation:**
- B2B SaaS companies with low churn (<3% monthly) trade for **4x to 8x ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** upon exit.
- Example:
  - 200 business customers paying $150/month = **$30,000 MRR ($360,000 ARR)**.
  - At a conservative 5x multiple, the business can sell for **$1.8 Million cash**.
- However, reaching $30,000 MRR typically requires 12 to 24 months of consistent customer discovery, software development, bug fixing, and cold outbound sales.

---

## 4. Step-by-Step Implementation Blueprint
1. **Step 1: The "Pain Cave" Discovery:**
   - Never write code first. Search Reddit (r/sysadmin, r/accounting, r/realtors) or B2B forums to find people complaining about software limitations (*"I hate doing X manually every Friday"*).
2. **Step 2: Pre-Selling Before Building:**
   - Create a Figma mockup or landing page.
   - Contact 30 prospective users and pitch: *"If I build this tool to automate X, will you pay $49/mo for the beta?"*
   - Do not write code until you have at least 5 signed letters of intent or PayPal pre-orders.
3. **Step 3: Rapid MVP Building:**
   - Use modern AI-assisted stacks: Next.js, Supabase, Tailwind, Stripe, and Cursor/Claude Code to ship the MVP in 3 weeks.
4. **Step 4: Customer Acquisition via Outbound:**
   - 80% cold email using Apollo.io and Smartlead, 20% organic content on LinkedIn.
5. **Step 5: The Exit:**
   - List the business on Acquire.com once ARR reaches $100k+ with steady growth.

### Critical Pitfalls & Hidden Traps:
- "The Developer's Trap": Spending 6 months writing beautiful code in isolation, launching to Twitter, and getting 0 paying users.
- High churn: If the tool has bugs or isn't mission-critical, businesses cancel after 30 days.
